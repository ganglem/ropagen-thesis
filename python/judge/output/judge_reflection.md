## RQ1: Influence of LLM Support Level on Document Quality

The data reveals a clear trade-off between the level of LLM scaffolding and the resulting factual quality of the generated documents. Counterintuitively, higher scaffolding does not reliably improve overall document quality; rather, it shifts the nature of the AI's performance. The Form mode, which provides the highest level of scaffolding, achieved the highest mean scores for legal correctness (3.595) and completeness (4.838). However, this rigid structure came at a severe cost to factual accuracy. The Form mode recorded the lowest scenario faithfulness (1.838) and the worst performance regarding hallucinations (hallucination_inverse = 1.649, where 1 indicates many hallucinations). 

Conversely, the Chat mode, featuring the lowest scaffolding, yielded the highest overall quality score (2.615) and the best scenario faithfulness (2.487), alongside the fewest hallucinations (hallucination_inverse = 2.538). The Ask mode, offering moderate scaffolding, generally fell between the two extremes in overall quality (2.486). This pattern suggests that while heavy scaffolding forces the LLM to generate legally plausible text, it simultaneously encourages the model to invent information to fill structural gaps, thereby degrading the document's actual utility for a specific scenario.

## RQ2a: Art. 30 Field Coverage Across Modes

Not all required Art. 30 GDPR fields are consistently present across the different interaction modes. The Form mode is unequivocally the most reliable for structural coverage, achieving a near-perfect mean completeness score of 4.838. The concrete examples highlight this reliability: even the worst-performing Form document (id=60) achieved a maximum completeness score of 5, demonstrating that the structured questionnaire effectively forces the LLM to populate all necessary fields. 

In contrast, as scaffolding decreases, so does structural reliability. The Ask mode achieved a moderate completeness mean of 3.838, while the Chat mode struggled significantly, scoring only 3.179. The worst Chat example (id=63) illustrates this vulnerability perfectly; it received a completeness score of 1 because it consisted merely of "empty placeholders for all critical GDPR requirements." Therefore, while freeform Chat interactions preserve factual integrity better, they frequently fail to independently generate the comprehensive structural coverage mandated by Art. 30 without explicit structural prompting.

## RQ2b: Confidence–Quality Gap

There is a substantial and concerning discrepancy between user confidence in the AI output and the actual quality of the documents, particularly regarding hallucinations. Novice participants reported high overall confidence across all modes, with the Ask mode yielding the highest overall survey mean (3.968), followed by Form (3.858) and Chat (3.797). 

This confidence is dangerously misplaced in the highly scaffolded modes. For instance, despite the Form mode producing the most severe hallucinations (hallucination_inverse = 1.649), users still reported a high legal confidence of 3.580. The gap is most visible in the worst Form example (id=60). Because the document looks structurally complete (completeness = 5), novices are likely lulled into a false sense of security, failing to recognize that the text is "entirely fabricated," inventing a company name ("OPENAI") and dangerously incorrect retention periods (6-10 years instead of 3 months). A similar gap appears in the worst Ask example (id=118), which hallucinates a fictitious company ("TechKG GmbH") and wrong retention periods, yet users in the Ask mode reported the highest trust (3.797). This indicates that novices conflate professional formatting and structural completeness with factual and legal accuracy.

## Synthesis

The findings highlight a critical "structure-hallucination trade-off" in LLM-assisted ROPA generation for novices. While highly scaffolded interfaces (Form) successfully guarantee the structural completeness required by Art. 30 GDPR, they paradoxically induce severe hallucinations that novice users fail to detect due to the documents' professional appearance. Conversely, freeform interactions (Chat) better preserve factual faithfulness but fail to reliably cover all mandatory legal fields. For novice users, relying on either extreme is risky; effective future tools must bridge this gap by combining the structural guarantees of form-based scaffolding with the factual grounding of chat-based generation, alongside explicit mechanisms to help users verify AI-generated claims.
