\section{Summary of Findings}
\label{sec:eval-summary}

The empirical findings can be summarised as follows. On the user-experience
instruments (RQ1), Form receives the highest overall SUS score ($78.62$
versus Ask $72.97$ and Chat $72.93$), the lowest overall R-TLX score
($5.50$ versus Ask and Chat $6.33$ each), and the largest share of first-place
preference votes ($53.6\,\%$). The overall SUS and R-TLX omnibus tests do
not reach the conventional significance threshold; the significant
sub-effects on the matched-pairs subset are confined to Form versus the
LLM-driven modes on Temporal Demand, Frustration, and re-use intent. Ask and
Chat are descriptively and inferentially indistinguishable from each other
on all aggregate user-experience measures.

On the Perceived AI Support items, the legal-basis confidence item ($5_{ai}$)
increases monotonically with LLM involvement (Form $3.58$, Ask $3.74$, Chat
$3.81$; aggregated $3.71$), while Form simultaneously yields the highest
re-use intent ($6_{ai}$) at $3.99$ versus Ask $3.55$ and Chat $3.42$; the
six-item overall score is essentially flat across modes. Free-text comments
characterise Form as fast and ergonomic, Ask as transparent but cumbersome,
and Chat as comfortable but opaque.

On the document-quality side (RQ2), the reference-based NLP metrics place
Form first on lexical recall (BERTScore Recall $0.923$ versus Ask $0.908$
and Chat $0.913$) and on the n-gram-based metrics, Chat first on semantic
similarity (SBERT $0.990$ versus $0.986$ for both other modes) and BLEU,
and Ask consistently last on every metric. The chapter-level breakdown
attributes Chat's BLEU and METEOR advantage primarily to the Retention
Periods and the Technical and Organisational Measures chapters. The
LLM-as-Judge inverts the BERTScore Recall ordering: Form leads on
Completeness ($4.84$) but lags on Scenario Faithfulness ($1.84$) and
Hallucination ($1.65$, inverse-coded), while Chat leads on the latter two
dimensions but trails on Completeness; Overall scores are clustered tightly
between $2.41$ and $2.62$ on the $1$--$5$ scale.

Taken together, the descriptive findings show that on the
self-report instruments (RQ1) the structured Form mode is rated
most usable, least demanding, and most preferred, while the two
LLM-driven modes (Ask and Chat) are descriptively
indistinguishable from each other on every aggregate user-experience
measure. On the document-quality side (RQ2) the LLM-driven Chat
mode achieves the highest semantic similarity to the reference and
the highest Faithfulness and (inverse) Hallucination ratings under
the LLM-as-Judge, while the structured Form mode achieves the
highest lexical recall and the highest Completeness rating. The
interpretation of these patterns against the research questions —
in particular the apparent gap between perceived AI support and
objective document quality — is taken up in
Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}.
