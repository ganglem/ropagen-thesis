\section{Document Quality — NLP Metrics}
\label{sec:eval-docquality}

The User Study (\S\ref{sec:eval-userstudy}) showed a stable
Form-first ordering on usability, workload, and overall preference,
with Chat second and Ask third. This section opens the
document-quality half of the evaluation by asking whether the same
ordering holds when the output text itself is compared against a
reference document. The reference-based metric suite mirrors the
one applied by von Schwerin et
al.\ \cite{schwerinSystematicComparisonOpen2024} in their
evaluation of LLM-generated ROPA data categories: five lexical
metrics (BLEU, ROUGE-1/2/L, METEOR) that count surface-form token
overlap, and three BERT-based metrics (BERTScore Precision /
Recall / F1) together with document-level SBERT cosine similarity
that compare semantic representations. All metrics are computed
against the single expert-authored Form-mode reference document
described in Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}. For every measure the
per-mode results are reported in the order Form, Ask, Chat,
followed by the aggregated value across all three modes. The
presentation proceeds in three steps:
\S\ref{sec:eval-nlp-doc} reports each metric at the document
level, treating the ROPA as a single block of text;
\S\ref{sec:eval-nlp-chapter} recomputes the same metrics at the
level of the six Article 30(1) ROPA chapters to identify where in
the document the per-mode differences originate; and
\S\ref{sec:eval-nlp-comparison} summarises the document-level and
chapter-level findings together, highlighting the similarities
between the two views as well as the gap in absolute scores.

\subsection{Document-Level NLP Metrics}
\label{sec:eval-nlp-doc}

Document-quality results are reported on the full retained set of
$113$ documents (Form $n = 37$, Ask $n = 37$, Chat $n = 39$) for
descriptive statistics, and on the strict matched-triple subset of
$n = 26$ for within-subjects significance tests. The descriptive
tables in this subsection report — per metric and per mode — the
full empirical distribution ($n$, min, Q1, median, Q3, max, mean,
SD) computed from the raw per-document scores in the pipeline
output \texttt{document\_results.csv}. The metrics are reported in
two groups: lexical metrics first, since they make the strictest
demands on surface form, followed by the BERT-based and
sentence-embedding metrics. Each metric is reported over the full
participant document, treating the ROPA as a single block of text.

\subsubsection{Lexical Metrics}
\label{ssec:nlp-doc-lexical}

Lexical metrics — BLEU, ROUGE, and METEOR — count surface-form
token overlap with the reference document; they reward word-level
fidelity but are blind to paraphrase, and are therefore reported
first because they make the strictest demands on surface form.
An LLM-generated document that covers the same content with
different wording will score lower here than under the
embedding-based metrics that follow.
Table~\ref{tab:nlp-doc-distrib-lexical} reports the full
distribution of the five lexical metrics (BLEU, ROUGE-1/2/L, METEOR)
per mode, plus the aggregated row across all $113$ documents.
Figure~\ref{fig:nlp-doc-boxplot} shows the corresponding box-plot
distributions.

\paragraph{BLEU.} BLEU \emph{(Bilingual Evaluation Understudy)} (\eqref{eq:bleu}) counts exact $n$-gram overlap with the reference and is therefore harsh on paraphrase: a document that says the same thing in different words scores low. It is precision-oriented (how much of what the candidate said also appears in the reference) with a brevity penalty, and the values reported here use $N = 4$ (matching unigrams through 4-grams).

Chat documents share slightly more exact phrasing with the
expert reference than Form or Ask documents do — a small
reversal of the Form-first ordering, but not at a magnitude
participants would notice on the page. For \emph{BLEU}, Form
yields a mean of $0.129$, Ask $0.127$, Chat $0.143$; aggregated
$0.133$. Chat scores marginally higher than Form and Ask, which
are essentially indistinguishable. Pairwise tests on the matched
subset find Chat $>$ Ask significant ($r = -0.578$, large
effect).

\paragraph{ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L.} ROUGE \emph{(Recall-Oriented Understudy for Gisting Evaluation)} (\eqref{eq:rouge1}--\eqref{eq:rougeL}) measures how much of the reference's wording the candidate recovers, in three flavours: unigrams (single words), bigrams (adjacent word pairs), and the longest common subsequence. Each is reported as F1 — the harmonic mean of precision (how much of what the candidate said belongs) and recall (how much of what should have been said is there) — so it is more permissive than BLEU in word order but still surface-form.

Looking at single-word overlap, Chat and Form documents recover the
reference's vocabulary at a similar rate, with Ask documents
trailing. For \emph{ROUGE-1}, Form yields $0.488$, Ask $0.465$,
Chat $0.511$; aggregated $0.488$. ROUGE-1 increases from Ask to
Form to Chat. Pairwise tests find Form $>$ Ask and Chat $>$ Ask
significant with large effects; Form versus Chat is not
significant.

When two-word phrases are compared, Form documents reproduce the
reference's exact wording most often, with Chat second and Ask
last. For \emph{ROUGE-2}, Form yields the highest mean ($0.306$),
Chat second ($0.291$), Ask lowest ($0.257$); aggregated $0.285$.
ROUGE-2 follows the Form $>$ Chat $>$ Ask ordering. Pairwise
tests find Form $>$ Ask significant ($r = +0.829$, large effect),
as is Chat $>$ Ask.

When the longest shared word sequence is what matters, Form and
Chat documents track the reference equally well, and Ask trails
both. For \emph{ROUGE-L}, Form ($0.388$) and Chat ($0.389$) are
essentially tied, with Ask trailing ($0.347$); aggregated $0.375$.
Pairwise tests find Form $>$ Ask and Chat $>$ Ask significant
with large effects.

\paragraph{METEOR.} METEOR \emph{(Metric for Evaluation of Translation with Explicit ORdering)} (\eqref{eq:meteor}) softens BLEU by allowing matches not just on identical tokens but also on stems and WordNet synonyms, so a document that paraphrases with related words is no longer punished as if it had said something unrelated. It is a fragmentation-penalised harmonic mean of unigram precision and recall, sitting between BLEU and ROUGE in strictness.

Form documents use the most reference-like terminology even when
synonyms are credited, with Chat second and Ask last. For
\emph{METEOR}, Form yields the highest mean ($0.412$), Chat second
($0.390$), Ask lowest ($0.365$); aggregated $0.389$. Pairwise
tests find Form $>$ Ask significant ($r = +0.812$, large effect);
the remaining pairwise differences do not reach the Bonferroni
threshold.

All five lexical Friedman tests on the matched $n = 26$ subset
are significant, with $\chi^2(2)$ values of $6.23$ (BLEU,
$p = 0.044$), $10.69$ (ROUGE-1, $p = 0.005$), $13.46$ (ROUGE-2,
$p = 0.001$), $11.31$ (ROUGE-L, $p = 0.004$), and $9.92$ (METEOR,
$p = 0.007$).

\paragraph{BERTScore Precision, Recall, F1.} BERTScore (\eqref{eq:bertscore-p}--\eqref{eq:bertscore-f1}) replaces $n$-gram matching with cosine similarities between contextual token embeddings, so paraphrase that shares meaning is credited even without surface-form overlap. \emph{Precision} measures how much of what the candidate said belongs (penalising spurious content), \emph{recall} measures how much of what should have been said is there (penalising omission), and \emph{F1} is their harmonic mean — both must be reasonable for F1 to be high, since the harmonic mean is dragged down by the smaller of the two.

When the question shifts from \emph{exact wording} to \emph{related
meaning}, Chat documents become the most on-topic — what they say
is most closely aligned with the reference, with very little
extraneous content. For \emph{BERTScore Precision}, Chat yields the
highest mean ($0.895$), Form second ($0.889$), Ask lowest
($0.886$); aggregated $0.890$. Pairwise tests find Form $>$ Ask
and Chat $>$ Ask significant.

Form documents recover the most of what the reference says — the
clearest finding of the entire metric pipeline. Where Chat trims to
what is on-topic, Form reproduces the reference's content
near-completely, while Ask leaves the most out. For \emph{BERTScore
Recall}, Form yields the highest mean ($0.923$), with Chat
($0.913$) and Ask ($0.908$) trailing; aggregated $0.915$.
Pairwise tests find Form $>$ Ask significant with the maximum
possible matched-pairs effect ($r = +1.000$; every one of the
$26$ matched pairs has Form $>$ Ask with no ties, which is the
source of the perfect rank-biserial rather than a tie-handling
artefact), and Form $>$ Chat also significant ($r = +0.687$,
large effect); Ask versus Chat is non-significant.

Form's content coverage and Chat's tighter focus balance out when
the two are combined: the two modes are effectively tied on overall
semantic similarity, with Ask trailing. For \emph{BERTScore F1},
Form yields the highest mean ($0.905$), Chat second ($0.904$), Ask
lowest ($0.897$); aggregated $0.902$. Pairwise tests find
Form $>$ Ask and Chat $>$ Ask significant with large effects;
Form versus Chat is non-significant on F1, reflecting the
trade-off between Form's higher Recall and Chat's higher
Precision.

\paragraph{SBERT (ModernBERT) cosine similarity.} Sentence-BERT cosine similarity (\eqref{eq:sbert}) compresses each document into a single embedding vector and reports how aligned the two vectors are, on a scale where $1$ is identical meaning and $0$ is unrelated. It is the most lenient of the metrics reported here: because the comparison is at the document level rather than the token level, two documents that cover the same topics with different wording typically obtain values close to $1$.

When the document is read as a whole, Chat reads the closest to the
reference — Chat documents cover the same overall ground in a way
no other mode quite matches. For \emph{SBERT cosine similarity
(ModernBERT)}, Chat yields the highest mean ($0.990$), with Form
and Ask tied at $0.986$; aggregated $0.987$. Pairwise tests find
Chat $>$ Form and Chat $>$ Ask both significant with large
effects.

All four BERT-based and sentence-embedding Friedman tests on the
matched subset are significant, with $\chi^2(2)$ values of
$18.54$ (BERTScore Precision, $p < 0.001$), $30.77$ (BERTScore
Recall, $p < 0.001$, $W = 0.410$ — the largest concordance effect
in the metric pipeline), $13.00$ (BERTScore F1, $p = 0.002$), and
$16.69$ (SBERT, $p < 0.001$).

In summary, on the document level the three modes split into the
patterns \emph{Form $\approx$ Chat $>$ Ask} (BLEU, ROUGE-1, ROUGE-L,
METEOR, BERTScore Precision, BERTScore F1), \emph{Form $>$ Chat $>$
Ask} on n-gram overlap of size two and BERTScore Recall, and
\emph{Chat $>$ Form $\approx$ Ask} on SBERT. Ask underperforms both
Form and Chat on every metric; Form and Chat trade leadership
depending on whether the metric emphasises lexical recall (Form
leads) or semantic-document-level fluency (Chat leads). The full
within-subjects test results are reproduced in
Appendix~\ref{app:inferential}.

\subsection{Chapter-Level NLP Metrics}
\label{sec:eval-nlp-chapter}

The document-level scores reported in \S\ref{sec:eval-nlp-doc}
collapse the entire ROPA into a single number per metric, which is
convenient for ranking modes but obscures where in the document the
differences originate. This subsection recomputes the same nine
metrics at the level of the six Article 30(1) ROPA chapters —
\emph{Verantwortliche Stelle und Kontaktdaten} (Controller and
Contact Details), \emph{Zwecke und Rechtsgrundlagen der Verarbeitung}
(Purposes and Legal Bases of Processing), \emph{Kategorien
personenbezogener Daten und Datenquellen} (Categories of Personal
Data and Sources), \emph{Betroffene Personen und Empfänger} (Data
Subjects and Recipients), \emph{Aufbewahrungsfristen und Löschung}
(Retention Periods and Deletion), and \emph{Technische und
organisatorische Maßnahmen} (Technical and Organisational Measures).
Chapter-level results characterise where in the document each mode
performs best. Following the same two-part presentation as the
document-level subsection, lexical metrics are reported first
(Tables~\ref{tab:nlp-chapter-bleu}--\ref{tab:nlp-chapter-meteor},
Figure~\ref{fig:chapter-nlp-boxplot}), followed by the BERT-based
and sentence-embedding metrics
(Tables~\ref{tab:nlp-chapter-bertp}--\ref{tab:nlp-chapter-sbert},
Figure~\ref{fig:chapter-bert-boxplot}). The descriptive source for
all chapter-level tables is the pipeline output
\texttt{chapter\_results.csv}.

almost entirely in \emph{Controller and Contact Details}, the one
ROPA chapter whose content is templated enough for exact $4$-gram
overlap to register: Form $0.179$, Ask $0.200$, Chat $0.166$
(aggregated $0.181$). \emph{Purposes and Legal Bases} and
\emph{Categories of Personal Data} collapse to zero across every
mode — the legal-text and category-list registers share no
$4$-grams with the reference even where they overlap semantically.
In the remaining three chapters BLEU sits in low single digits,
with \emph{Retention and Deletion} producing the clearest
chapter-level Chat lead in the lexical metric suite (Chat $0.068$
vs Form $0.038$, Ask $0.023$; aggregated $0.044$) and
\emph{Technical and Organisational Measures} echoing it on a
smaller scale (Chat $0.091$ vs Form $0.078$, Ask $0.070$;
aggregated $0.080$). Friedman tests on the matched $n = 26$
subset confirm significant mode differences on \emph{Controller
and Contact} ($\chi^2(2) = 9.92$, $p = 0.007$), \emph{Retention}
($\chi^2(2) = 14.52$, $p < 0.001$), and \emph{T\&O Measures}
($\chi^2(2) = 18.93$, $p < 0.001$, $W = 0.361$ strong); ch2 and
ch3 are degenerate (every BLEU score is zero). Bonferroni-
corrected pairwise tests confirm Form $>$ Ask and Chat $>$ Ask on
\emph{Retention} and \emph{T\&O} (large effects), and
\emph{reverse} the doc-level direction on \emph{Controller}: Ask
$>$ Chat is significant there ($r = +0.631$, large) — the only
chapter in the lexical pipeline where Ask outscores Chat under
Bonferroni.

\paragraph{ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L.} Single-word recall
reproduces the same chapter hierarchy as BLEU but lifts every
chapter off the floor.
\emph{Controller and Contact} again leads (Form $0.550$, Ask
$0.605$, Chat $0.559$; aggregated $0.571$), with Ask edging the
other two modes because the address-block content offers little
room for paraphrase. \emph{Purposes and Legal Bases} (aggregated
$0.028$) and \emph{Categories of Personal Data} (aggregated
$0.070$) remain very low. \emph{Technical and Organisational
Measures} is the second-highest chapter (aggregated $0.331$), and
\emph{Retention and Deletion} is where Chat's chapter-level
advantage is most visible at the unigram level (Chat $0.314$ vs
Form $0.252$, Ask $0.212$; aggregated $0.260$). Friedman tests
find significant ROUGE-1 mode differences on \emph{Controller}
($\chi^2(2) = 9.96$, $p = 0.007$), \emph{Data Subjects and
Recipients} ($\chi^2(2) = 9.00$, $p = 0.011$), and
\emph{Retention} ($\chi^2(2) = 13.00$, $p = 0.002$); pairwise
tests show Form $>$ Ask on Retention ($r = +0.846$, large), Chat
$>$ Ask on Data Subjects and Retention (both large), and Chat
$>$ Form on Data Subjects ($r = -0.590$, large).

The bigram view sharpens the same picture.
\emph{Controller and Contact} stays at the top (Form $0.363$, Ask
$0.430$, Chat $0.365$; aggregated $0.386$), and \emph{Purposes
and Legal Bases} stays at the floor (aggregated $0.011$). Where
ROUGE-1 left \emph{Retention and Deletion} as a moderate Chat
lead, ROUGE-2 separates the modes more sharply (Chat $0.168$ vs
Form $0.134$, Ask $0.087$; aggregated $0.131$) — Chat is the only
mode that reproduces multi-word retention phrasing from the
reference at any volume. \emph{Technical and Organisational
Measures} repeats the pattern on a smaller margin (Chat $0.197$
vs Form $0.166$, Ask $0.163$; aggregated $0.176$). Friedman tests
find significant ROUGE-2 mode differences on \emph{Data Subjects}
($\chi^2(2) = 8.08$, $p = 0.018$), \emph{Retention}
($\chi^2(2) = 11.62$, $p = 0.003$), and \emph{T\&O Measures}
($\chi^2(2) = 14.08$, $p < 0.001$); pairwise tests show Form $>$
Ask on Retention ($r = +0.715$, large) and Chat $>$ Ask on Data
Subjects, Retention, and T\&O (all large).

The longest-shared-sequence variant tracks ROUGE-1 closely. \emph{Controller and Contact} dominates (Form
$0.518$, Ask $0.576$, Chat $0.511$; aggregated $0.534$),
\emph{Purposes} (aggregated $0.027$) and \emph{Categories of
Personal Data} (aggregated $0.070$) stay near the floor, and
Chat's chapter-level advantage on \emph{Retention and Deletion}
persists (Chat $0.283$ vs Form $0.218$, Ask $0.183$; aggregated
$0.229$). \emph{Technical and Organisational Measures} reinforces
the same direction on a smaller margin (Chat $0.285$ vs Form
$0.237$, Ask $0.246$; aggregated $0.257$). Friedman tests find
significant ROUGE-L mode differences on four chapters —
\emph{Controller}, \emph{Data Subjects}, \emph{Retention}, and
\emph{T\&O Measures} ($\chi^2(2) \in [8.18, 13.46]$, all $p \leq
0.017$). Pairwise tests reproduce the BLEU reversal: Ask $>$ Chat
on \emph{Controller} ($r = +0.567$, large), and confirm Chat $>$
Form / Chat $>$ Ask on Data Subjects (both large), Form $>$ Ask
and Chat $>$ Ask on Retention (both large), and Chat $>$ Ask on
T\&O.

\paragraph{METEOR.} Once stems and synonyms are credited the
chapter range compresses. \emph{Controller and Contact} retains
the top position (Form $0.456$, Ask $0.506$, Chat $0.453$;
aggregated $0.471$), and \emph{Purposes and Legal Bases} remains
very low (aggregated $0.027$, with all three modes below $0.04$).
The mid-table chapters lift the most: \emph{Data Subjects and
Recipients} climbs to aggregated $0.225$ on the strength of
synonym credit, and shows the clearest Chat lead on this metric
(Chat $0.270$ vs Form $0.199$, Ask $0.203$). \emph{Retention and
Deletion} (Chat $0.362$ vs Form $0.332$, Ask $0.245$; aggregated
$0.314$) and \emph{Technical and Organisational Measures} (Chat
$0.305$ vs Form $0.295$, Ask $0.280$; aggregated $0.293$) again
favour Chat. Friedman tests find significant METEOR mode
differences on \emph{Data Subjects} ($\chi^2(2) = 9.77$, $p =
0.008$), \emph{Retention} ($\chi^2(2) = 16.69$, $p < 0.001$,
$W = 0.321$ strong), and \emph{T\&O Measures}
($\chi^2(2) = 6.46$, $p = 0.040$). Pairwise tests confirm Chat
$>$ Form and Chat $>$ Ask on Data Subjects (large), Form $>$ Ask
and Chat $>$ Ask on Retention (large), and Form $>$ Ask on T\&O.

Across the five lexical metrics the across-chapter signal is
dominated by two end-points: \emph{Controller and Contact
Details} at the top — the chapter whose content is templated
enough for surface overlap to register, and the one chapter
where the structured Ask dialogue beats Chat — and \emph{Purposes
and Legal Bases} at the floor, where free-form legal formulation
pulls every score toward zero. Chat's chapter-level lead, where
it appears, concentrates on \emph{Retention and Deletion} and
\emph{Technical and Organisational Measures}; Form's lead on
\emph{Retention} on the recall-heavy metrics (ROUGE-2, METEOR).
Of the $28$ testable lexical (metric, chapter) cells (BLEU on
\emph{Purposes} and \emph{Categories} is degenerate), $16$
Friedman omnibus tests reach $\alpha = 0.05$, with
\emph{Retention and Deletion} significant on all five lexical
metrics, \emph{T\&O Measures} on four of five, and \emph{Purposes
and Legal Bases} significant on none.

\paragraph{BERTScore Precision, Recall, F1.} The embedding-based
view compresses the across-chapter range substantially: no chapter
sits at zero, and the highest-to-lowest gap shrinks to roughly
$0.3$. \emph{Controller and Contact Details} (aggregated $0.857$)
and \emph{Technical and Organisational Measures} (aggregated

the weakest chapter (aggregated $0.566$) but no longer collapses
to the floor. Chat is the per-mode leader on three of the four
mid-and-lower-ranked chapters — \emph{Categories of Personal
Data} ($0.698$ vs Form $0.670$, Ask $0.690$), \emph{Data Subjects
and Recipients} ($0.712$ vs Form $0.686$, Ask $0.700$), and

\emph{Personal Data Categories} ($\chi^2(2) = 15.46$, $p < 0.001$),
\emph{Data Subjects} ($\chi^2(2) = 12.00$, $p = 0.002$),
\emph{Retention} ($\chi^2(2) = 9.54$, $p = 0.008$), and
\emph{T\&O Measures} ($\chi^2(2) = 9.31$, $p = 0.010$). Pairwise
tests confirm Chat $>$ Form (large) on Categories and Data
Subjects, and Chat $>$ Ask (large) on Categories, Data Subjects,
and Retention.

Form's document-level recall
advantage carries through to the chapter level on most chapters.
Form leads on \emph{Categories of Personal Data} ($0.850$ vs Ask
$0.849$, Chat $0.847$ — narrow but consistent with the
document-level pattern) and on \emph{Data Subjects and
Recipients} ($0.851$ vs Ask $0.838$, Chat $0.836$). Chat reclaims
\emph{Retention and Deletion} ($0.897$ vs Form $0.890$, Ask
$0.879$; aggregated $0.889$). \emph{Controller and Contact}
(aggregated $0.882$) and \emph{Technical and Organisational
Measures} (aggregated $0.888$) are tight three-way ties.
\emph{Purposes and Legal Bases} is again the floor (aggregated
$0.666$), and is the only chapter where Form is not at the front
(Form $0.670$, Ask $0.667$, Chat $0.662$ — all clustered). Of
the six chapters, only \emph{Retention} reaches Friedman
significance on Recall ($\chi^2(2) = 18.77$, $p < 0.001$,
$W = 0.361$ strong); pairwise tests confirm Form $>$ Ask and
Chat $>$ Ask there, both large.

The harmonic mean restores the
by-now-familiar chapter ordering — \emph{Controller and Contact}
(aggregated $0.869$) and \emph{Technical and Organisational
Measures} (aggregated $0.871$) at the top, \emph{Purposes and
Legal Bases} at the floor (aggregated $0.612$). Chat's precision
advantage carries through to a chapter-level F1 lead on
\emph{Retention and Deletion} (Chat $0.867$ vs Form $0.850$, Ask
$0.847$; aggregated $0.855$) and \emph{Technical and
Organisational Measures} (Chat $0.878$ vs Form $0.864$, Ask
$0.870$; aggregated $0.871$), reproducing in miniature the
document-level pattern where Chat and Form trade leadership on
F1. Friedman tests find significant F1 mode differences on
\emph{Controller} ($\chi^2(2) = 6.23$, $p = 0.044$),
\emph{Categories} ($\chi^2(2) = 19.39$, $p < 0.001$, $W = 0.373$
strong), and \emph{Retention} ($\chi^2(2) = 12.08$, $p = 0.002$);
pairwise tests confirm Chat $>$ Form and Chat $>$ Ask on
Categories (both large) and Chat $>$ Ask on Retention.

\paragraph{SBERT cosine similarity (ModernBERT and MiniLM).}
Under the ModernBERT encoder, the most permissive of the metrics
saturates near the ceiling on the templated chapters:
\emph{Controller and Contact} (aggregated $0.976$) and
\emph{Technical and Organisational Measures} (aggregated $0.971$)
are essentially indistinguishable from the reference at the
embedding level. \emph{Retention and Deletion} follows close
behind (aggregated $0.949$) with Chat opening a small
chapter-level lead (Chat $0.955$ vs Form $0.945$, Ask $0.946$).
The only chapter where ModernBERT SBERT drops meaningfully is
\emph{Purposes and Legal Bases} (aggregated $0.735$), echoing the
lexical collapse at sentence-embedding scale. Friedman tests find
significant ModernBERT SBERT mode differences on
\emph{Purposes} ($\chi^2(2) = 7.15$, $p = 0.028$),
\emph{Categories} ($\chi^2(2) = 14.08$, $p < 0.001$),
\emph{Retention} ($\chi^2(2) = 6.23$, $p = 0.044$), and
\emph{T\&O Measures} ($\chi^2(2) = 7.00$, $p = 0.030$); pairwise
tests confirm Chat $>$ Form on Categories and T\&O (large) and
Chat $>$ Ask on Retention (large).

The smaller \texttt{all-MiniLM-L6-v2} encoder serves as a
robustness check: a weaker model with less semantic capacity
spreads scores across a wider range and is harder to saturate.
The across-chapter ordering survives the encoder change
unchanged — \emph{Technical and Organisational Measures}
(aggregated $0.710$) and \emph{Controller and Contact}
(aggregated $0.687$) sit at the top, with \emph{Purposes and
Legal Bases} again at the floor (aggregated $0.298$). The
chapter-level Chat advantage on \emph{Retention and Deletion}
(Chat $0.566$ vs Form $0.516$, Ask $0.474$) and on \emph{Data
Subjects and Recipients} (Chat $0.502$ vs Form $0.399$, Ask
$0.414$) is preserved and in fact visible at larger absolute
margins than ModernBERT registered. The one chapter where the
encoder choice changes the mode ranking is \emph{Controller and
Contact}, where Ask edges the other two modes under MiniLM
($0.736$ vs Form $0.663$, Chat $0.662$) — consistent with the
ROUGE-1/L pattern at the lexical level, where Ask also leads on
the address-block content. Friedman tests find significant MiniLM
mode differences on \emph{Data Subjects}
($\chi^2(2) = 23.15$, $p < 0.001$, $W = 0.445$ strong — the
largest chapter-level effect observed in the entire NLP metric
pipeline) and \emph{Retention} ($\chi^2(2) = 9.31$, $p = 0.010$);
pairwise tests confirm Chat $>$ Form and Chat $>$ Ask on Data
Subjects (both large) and Chat $>$ Ask on Retention (large).

Under the embedding-based metrics the across-chapter range
compresses — no zero floor — but the rank-ordering of chapters
is preserved across all four BERT-based metrics and both SBERT
encoders: \emph{Controller and Contact} and \emph{Technical and
Organisational Measures} occupy the top, \emph{Purposes and
Legal Bases} the bottom. Chat's chapter-level advantage
continues to concentrate on \emph{Retention and Deletion} and
\emph{T\&O Measures}, the same two chapters where it led at the
lexical level; the smaller MiniLM encoder makes that advantage
easier to see by widening the absolute margins. Of the $30$
BERT-side (metric, chapter) cells, $14$ Friedman omnibus tests
reach $\alpha = 0.05$, with \emph{Retention and Deletion}
significant on every BERT-based metric and \emph{Personal Data
Categories} significant on BERTScore Precision, BERTScore F1,
and both SBERT encoders. Across the full $60$-cell (metric,
chapter) family, $30$ Friedman tests reach uncorrected
$\alpha = 0.05$ and $7$ survive a family-wise Bonferroni
correction at $\alpha \approx 0.000833$; the chapter-level
inferential layer therefore confirms that the doc-level Form-Ask-
Chat ordering holds in specific chapter cells rather than washing
out as noise.

\subsection{Summary of Document-Level and Chapter-Level NLP Metrics}
\label{sec:eval-nlp-comparison}

Read together, the two granularities mostly reinforce one another
and disagree only on absolute level. The clearest similarity is
that the per-mode ordering is preserved at both resolutions: Form
leads on recall-oriented and bigram-sensitive measures, most
strikingly on \emph{BERTScore Recall} where the document-level
matched-pairs effect is the maximum possible ($r = +1.000$, every
one of the $26$ matched pairs has Form $>$ Ask with no ties);
Chat leads on embedding-based measures (\emph{BERTScore
Precision}, \emph{SBERT}); and Ask trails on every metric. The
chapter-level view shows the same pattern in localised form —
Chat's chapter-level lead on \emph{Retention and Deletion} and
\emph{Technical and Organisational Measures} is precisely what
produces its document-level edge on semantic metrics, and Form's
chapter-level recall edge on \emph{Categories of Personal Data}
and \emph{Data Subjects and Recipients} is what produces its
document-level recall lead. The two views are not in tension;
they are the same finding seen at different resolutions. The
chapter-level inferential layer confirms this descriptive
agreement: $30$ of $60$ Friedman tests on the matched $n = 26$
subset reach $\alpha = 0.05$, with \emph{Retention and Deletion}
significant on every metric and the Form $>$ Ask gap localised to
the operational chapters (Retention, T\&O) while Chat $>$ Form
concentrates on the people-and-data chapters (Categories, Data
Subjects). The chapter breakdown also exposes one direction
reversal hidden at the document level: on \emph{Controller and
Contact}, Ask outscores Chat on BLEU and ROUGE-L (large effects,
$r \approx +0.6$ under Bonferroni) — the only chapter in the
lexical pipeline where Ask beats Chat, consistent with the
address-block content being templated enough for guided dialogue
to outperform freeform.

The contrast lies in absolute scores. Document-level means are
consistently higher than the unweighted average of chapter-level
means (Table~\ref{tab:doc-vs-chapter}); the largest gap is on
\emph{ROUGE-1} ($\Delta \approx 0.26$), and even on the
embedding metrics the gap is non-trivial ($\Delta \approx 0.11$
on BERTScore F1). The cause is mechanical rather than
substantive: the document-level calculation pools token overlap
across all six ROPA chapters and dilutes the chapter-level
zero-overlap regions, above all \emph{Purposes and Legal Bases},
the universal floor across every metric. Chapter-level scoring is
the more honest signal of \emph{where} the modes differ;
document-level scoring is the cleaner signal for ranking modes
\emph{overall}.

Taken together, the reference-based metrics neither confirm nor
cleanly contradict the Form-first ordering reported by the
user-experience instruments
(\S\ref{sec:eval-sus}--\S\ref{sec:eval-freeform}). The
quantitative split is closer to a trade-off: Form documents
recover more of what the reference \emph{says}, Chat documents
stay closer to what the reference \emph{means}, and Ask
documents trail on every front.
