\section{Document Quality — Reference-Based NLP Metrics}
\label{sec:eval-nlp}

Document-quality results are reported on the full retained set of $113$
documents (Form $n = 37$, Ask $n = 37$, Chat $n = 39$) for descriptive
statistics, and on the strict matched-triple subset of $n = 26$ for
within-subjects significance tests. All metrics are computed against the
single expert-authored Form-mode reference document described in
Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology}. For each metric, the per-mode results are
reported in the order Form, Ask, Chat, followed by the aggregated value
across all three modes.

deviation per metric per mode and the aggregated mean.

For \emph{BLEU}, Form yields a mean of $0.129$, Ask $0.127$, Chat $0.143$;
the aggregated mean is $0.133$. Chat scores marginally higher than Form and
Ask, which are essentially indistinguishable. The Friedman omnibus on the
matched subset is significant ($\chi^2(2) = 6.23$, $p = 0.044$); the
significant pairwise difference is Chat $>$ Ask ($r = -0.578$, large effect).

For \emph{ROUGE-1}, Form yields $0.488$, Ask $0.465$, Chat $0.511$;
aggregated $0.488$. ROUGE-1 increases from Ask to Form to Chat. The Friedman
omnibus is significant ($\chi^2(2) = 10.69$, $p = 0.005$); Form $>$ Ask and
Chat $>$ Ask are significant pairwise differences with large effects, while
Form versus Chat is not significant.

For \emph{ROUGE-2}, Form yields the highest mean ($0.306$), Chat second
($0.291$), Ask lowest ($0.257$); aggregated $0.285$. ROUGE-2 follows the
Form $>$ Chat $>$ Ask ordering. The Friedman omnibus is significant
($\chi^2(2) = 13.46$, $p = 0.001$); Form $>$ Ask is significant
($r = +0.829$, large effect), as is Chat $>$ Ask.

For \emph{ROUGE-L}, Form ($0.388$) and Chat ($0.389$) are essentially tied,
with Ask trailing ($0.347$); aggregated $0.375$. The Friedman omnibus is
significant ($\chi^2(2) = 11.31$, $p = 0.004$); Form $>$ Ask and Chat $>$ Ask
are significant pairwise differences with large effects.

For \emph{METEOR}, Form yields the highest mean ($0.412$), Chat second
($0.390$), Ask lowest ($0.365$); aggregated $0.389$. The Friedman omnibus is
significant ($\chi^2(2) = 9.92$, $p = 0.007$); Form $>$ Ask is significant
($r = +0.812$, large effect), and the remaining pairwise differences do not
reach the Bonferroni threshold.

For \emph{BERTScore Precision}, Chat yields the highest mean ($0.895$), Form
second ($0.889$), Ask lowest ($0.886$); aggregated $0.890$. The Friedman
omnibus is significant ($\chi^2(2) = 18.54$, $p < 0.001$); Form $>$ Ask and
Chat $>$ Ask are significant.

For \emph{BERTScore Recall}, Form yields the highest mean ($0.923$), with
Chat ($0.913$) and Ask ($0.908$) trailing; aggregated $0.915$. The Friedman
omnibus is significant ($\chi^2(2) = 30.77$, $p < 0.001$, $W = 0.410$, the
largest effect observed in the metric pipeline); Form $>$ Ask is significant
with the maximum possible matched-pairs effect ($r = +1.000$), and Form $>$
Chat is also significant ($r = +0.687$, large effect). Ask versus Chat is
non-significant.

For \emph{BERTScore F1}, Form yields the highest mean ($0.905$), Chat second
($0.904$), Ask lowest ($0.897$); aggregated $0.902$. The Friedman omnibus is
significant ($\chi^2(2) = 13.00$, $p = 0.002$); Form $>$ Ask and Chat $>$ Ask
are significant with large effects. Form versus Chat is non-significant on
F1, reflecting the trade-off between Form's higher Recall and Chat's higher
Precision.

For \emph{SBERT cosine similarity (ModernBERT)}, Chat yields the highest
mean ($0.990$), with Form and Ask tied at $0.986$; aggregated $0.987$. The
Friedman omnibus is significant ($\chi^2(2) = 16.69$, $p < 0.001$); Chat $>$
Form and Chat $>$ Ask are both significant with large effects.

In summary, on the document level the three modes split into the patterns
\emph{Form $\approx$ Chat $>$ Ask} (BLEU, ROUGE-1, ROUGE-L, METEOR, BERTScore
Precision, BERTScore F1), \emph{Form $>$ Chat $>$ Ask} on n-gram overlap of
size two and BERTScore Recall, and \emph{Chat $>$ Form $\approx$ Ask} on
SBERT. Ask underperforms both Form and Chat on every metric; Form and Chat
trade leadership depending on whether the metric emphasises lexical recall
(Form leads) or semantic-document-level fluency (Chat leads). The full
within-subjects test results are reproduced in
Appendix~\ref{app:inferential}.

\subsection{Chapter-Level Results}
\label{ssec:nlp-chapter}

The same metrics are recomputed at the level of the six Article 30(1) ROPA
sections — \emph{Verantwortliche Stelle und Kontaktdaten} (Controller and
Contact Details), \emph{Zwecke und Rechtsgrundlagen der Verarbeitung}
(Purposes and Legal Bases of Processing), \emph{Kategorien personenbezogener
Daten und Datenquellen} (Categories of Personal Data and Sources),
\emph{Betroffene Personen und Empfänger} (Data Subjects and Recipients),
\emph{Aufbewahrungsfristen und Löschung} (Retention Periods and Deletion),
and \emph{Technische und organisatorische Maßnahmen} (Technical and
Organisational Measures). Chapter-level results characterise where in the
document each mode performs best.

For \emph{Controller and Contact Details}, all three modes score similarly
on BERTScore F1 (Form $0.867$, Ask $0.875$, Chat $0.867$; aggregated
$0.869$) and on BLEU (Form $0.179$, Ask $0.200$, Chat $0.166$; aggregated
$0.181$). METEOR is highest in Ask ($0.506$) compared with Form ($0.456$)
and Chat ($0.453$); aggregated $0.471$. This chapter contains the most
templated content (company name, address, DPO contact), and the per-mode
spread is correspondingly small.

For \emph{Purposes and Legal Bases of Processing}, BERTScore F1 is uniformly
the lowest of the six chapters across all modes (Form $0.610$, Ask $0.614$,
Chat $0.611$; aggregated $0.612$), and BLEU is identically zero in every
mode. METEOR is also extremely low (Form $0.034$, Ask $0.034$, Chat $0.015$;
aggregated $0.027$). This chapter is dominated by free-text legal
formulation; the reference document and the participant documents share
almost no surface n-grams, even where the underlying content overlaps.

For \emph{Categories of Personal Data and Sources}, BERTScore F1 is
relatively flat (Form $0.749$, Ask $0.761$, Chat $0.765$; aggregated
$0.759$). BLEU is uniformly zero across modes. METEOR shows the largest
spread of any chapter on this metric, with Ask the highest ($0.140$),
Chat second ($0.101$), and Form lowest ($0.079$); aggregated $0.106$.
The LLM-driven modes therefore share more lexical content with the reference
on data categories than Form does.

For \emph{Data Subjects and Recipients}, BERTScore F1 is broadly comparable
across modes (Form $0.760$, Ask $0.762$, Chat $0.769$; aggregated $0.764$).
METEOR is highest in Chat ($0.270$), with Form ($0.199$) and Ask ($0.203$)
trailing; aggregated $0.225$. BLEU is essentially zero across all three
modes ($0.008$ to $0.014$); aggregated $0.012$.

For \emph{Retention Periods and Deletion}, Chat scores highest on every
metric: BERTScore F1 $0.867$ versus Form $0.850$ and Ask $0.847$
(aggregated $0.855$); BLEU $0.068$ versus Form $0.038$ and Ask $0.023$
(aggregated $0.044$); METEOR $0.362$ versus Form $0.332$ and Ask $0.245$
(aggregated $0.314$). This chapter shows the largest Chat advantage on
chapter-level metrics: the conversational mode appears to elicit the most
reference-aligned wording for retention specifications.

For \emph{Technical and Organisational Measures}, Chat again leads on
BERTScore F1 ($0.878$) and BLEU ($0.091$), with Ask second on F1 ($0.870$)
and Form third ($0.864$); aggregated F1 $0.871$, aggregated BLEU $0.080$.
METEOR is comparable across modes (Form $0.295$, Ask $0.280$, Chat $0.305$;
aggregated $0.293$). All three modes score relatively high on this chapter
because the underlying content (encryption, role-based access, backup
schedules) is the most stylistically conventional in the document.

A direct comparison of the document-level and chapter-level means is
provided by Table~\ref{tab:doc-vs-chapter}. Document-level means are
substantially higher than the cross-chapter means for every metric: for
example, the aggregated document-level BERTScore F1 of $0.902$ exceeds the
average chapter-level F1 of $0.788$ by approximately $0.114$. The same
gap is observable for the lexical metrics, and most pronounced for ROUGE-1
($\Delta \approx 0.26$). This is consistent with the document-level
calculation pooling token overlap across all six chapters, which dilutes
chapter-specific zero-overlap regions (notably the Purposes and Legal
Bases chapter) and pushes the aggregate scores upward.
