\section{Summary of Findings}
\label{sec:eval-summary}

This section consolidates what the user-study and document-quality
results say about how the three modes actually behaved in
participants' hands. Three orderings recur across the chapter, and
they are not all the same ordering. Form leads on how the system
\emph{felt} (usability, workload, the mode participants would pick
again) and on how much of the expert reference's wording the
documents \emph{recovered} (lexical NLP metrics, BERTScore Recall,
judge-rated Completeness). Chat leads on how on-topic and
scenario-faithful the documents \emph{were} (BERTScore F1, SBERT
cosine, judge-rated Faithfulness, judge-rated low Hallucination).
Ask sits in the middle on most measures and is descriptively last
on every reference-based NLP metric. The sections below summarise
each measurement family in turn; the interpretation is taken up in
Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}.

\paragraph{Usability (SUS).}
Form felt easier, less complex, and less cumbersome than the
LLM-driven modes; the items participants treat as raw operability
(ease of use, learnability, cumbersomeness) all favour Form, while
the items that level out across modes (technical-support need,
inconsistency, prior learning) suggest none of the three is
fundamentally hard to operate. Within-system confidence (item~9) is
also flat. Concretely: ease-of-use item~3 Form $4.38$ vs.\ Ask
$4.01$ vs.\ Chat $3.99$; learnability item~7 Form $4.30$ vs.\
$4.00$/$4.03$; cumbersomeness item~8 Form $1.65$ vs.\ Ask $2.16$
vs.\ Chat $2.29$; confidence item~9 essentially flat
($3.83$/$3.84$/$3.90$). The aggregated Friedman test on the
per-mode SUS aggregate is non-significant
($\chi^2(2) = 5.51$, $p = 0.064$, $W = 0.040$);
Bonferroni-corrected Wilcoxon post-hoc tests yield a significant
Form-versus-Ask difference
($W = 578.5$, $p = 0.013$, $r = +0.368$), a Form-versus-Chat
difference at the threshold ($p = 0.018$), and no Ask-versus-Chat
difference ($p = 0.666$).

\paragraph{Workload (NASA R-TLX).}
Form felt the lightest on every demand axis the participants were
asked about — least mentally demanding, least frustrating, least
hurried — and the gap is widest on Frustration and Temporal
Demand. Self-rated Performance is the one axis where the modes are
indistinguishable: participants do not feel they did better in one
mode than in another. Concretely: Mental Demand $5.77$ vs.\
$6.80$/$6.36$, Physical Demand $2.77$ vs.\ $3.54$/$3.26$, Temporal
Demand $4.16$ vs.\ $4.97$/$5.54$, Effort $5.68$ vs.\ $6.39$/$6.32$,
Frustration $5.88$ vs.\ $7.58$/$7.13$, Performance essentially flat
($8.71$/$8.68$/$9.39$). The aggregated Friedman test across the six
subscales is non-significant ($\chi^2(2) = 5.24$, $p = 0.073$,
$W = 0.038$); Temporal Demand
($\chi^2(2) = 12.77$, $p = 0.002$) and Frustration
($\chi^2(2) = 8.93$, $p = 0.012$) reach significance individually,
with significant pairwise contrasts confined to Form versus the
LLM-driven modes (medium-to-large effects, $|r| = 0.36$ to $0.57$);
Ask versus Chat is non-significant on every subscale.

\paragraph{Perceived AI Support.}
Asked specifically about the AI's contribution, participants split
their answers into two camps. They credit the LLM-driven modes for
\emph{understanding why} suggestions were made (transparency, trust,
legal-basis confidence) — and they credit Form for \emph{getting
the job done} (efficiency, willingness to use again). Confidence
that the legal basis is correct rises monotonically with LLM
involvement (Form $3.58$, Ask $3.74$, Chat $3.81$), while
willingness to use the mode again falls in the same direction
(Form $3.99$, Ask $3.55$, Chat $3.42$). Transparency item~3 is
where Ask leads ($4.16$ vs.\ Form $3.51$ vs.\ Chat $3.75$) — it is
the only mode that explicitly explains each suggestion. The
six-item aggregate is flat across modes (Friedman $\chi^2(2) =
1.05$, $p = 0.592$); legal-basis confidence is non-significant on
its own ($\chi^2(2) = 3.76$, $p = 0.152$); the re-use item is
significant ($\chi^2(2) = 11.12$, $p = 0.004$, $W = 0.081$;
Form-vs-Chat $r = +0.543$, large effect).

\paragraph{Mode Preference.}
When asked to commit to one mode at the end of the session, more
than half of participants put Form first; Chat is the next-most
common first choice; Ask is rarely picked first. Concretely, on
$n = 69$: Form $53.6\,\%$ of first-place votes ($37$), Chat
$29.0\,\%$ ($20$), Ask $17.4\,\%$ ($12$); mean ranks $1.78$ /
$2.07$ / $2.14$. The chi-square goodness-of-fit against a uniform
distribution is significant
($\chi^2(2, N = 69) = 14.17$, $p < 0.001$, Cramér's $V = 0.320$,
medium effect); the Friedman test on the full rank distribution is
non-significant
($\chi^2(2) = 5.07$, $p = 0.079$).

\paragraph{NLP Metrics (document level, $n = 113$).}
The reference-based picture is genuinely split. Form documents
\emph{recover} the most of the reference's content (highest Recall,
highest bigram and phrase overlap, highest synonym-aware overlap),
Chat documents \emph{stay closest} to what the reference is about
when meaning is what counts (highest Precision, highest SBERT,
highest BLEU), and Ask documents trail on every reference-based
metric. Means by mode (Form / Ask / Chat / aggregate): BLEU
$0.129$ / $0.127$ / $0.143$ / $0.133$; ROUGE-1
$0.488$ / $0.465$ / $0.511$ / $0.488$; ROUGE-2
$0.306$ / $0.257$ / $0.291$ / $0.285$; ROUGE-L
$0.388$ / $0.347$ / $0.389$ / $0.375$; METEOR
$0.412$ / $0.365$ / $0.390$ / $0.389$; BERTScore Precision
$0.889$ / $0.886$ / $0.895$ / $0.890$; BERTScore Recall
$0.923$ / $0.908$ / $0.913$ / $0.915$; BERTScore F1
$0.905$ / $0.897$ / $0.904$ / $0.902$; SBERT
$0.986$ / $0.986$ / $0.990$ / $0.987$. Aggregated Friedman tests are
significant for every metric (largest effect: BERTScore Recall,
$\chi^2(2) = 30.77$, $p < 0.001$, $W = 0.410$); pairwise post-hoc
results back the same split — Form dominates BERTScore Recall (Form
vs.\ Ask $r = +1.000$; Form vs.\ Chat $r = +0.687$) and ROUGE-2
(Form vs.\ Ask $r = +0.829$); Chat dominates SBERT (Chat vs.\ Form
and Chat vs.\ Ask significant, large effects); Ask is last on every
metric.

\paragraph{LLM-as-a-Judge ($1$--$5$ scale).}
The judge tells the same story the metrics do — and a sharper
version of it. Form documents are the most complete (they hit the
Article 30(1) checklist) but also the most prone to fabrication
(they invent facts the scenario does not support). Chat documents
are the most faithful to the actual scenario but the least complete.
Ask sits between the two extremes on every dimension. The Overall
verdict collapses these contrasts into a tight cluster, with Chat
edging ahead. Means by mode (Form / Ask / Chat / aggregate):
Completeness $4.84$ / $3.84$ / $3.18$ / $3.94$; Scenario
Faithfulness $1.84$ / $2.30$ / $2.49$ / $2.21$; Legal Correctness
$3.59$ / $3.43$ / $3.15$ / $3.39$; Hallucination (inverse)
$1.65$ / $2.40$ / $2.54$ / $2.20$; Overall
$2.41$ / $2.49$ / $2.62$ / $2.50$. Form has the lowest standard
deviation on Completeness ($0.55$ vs.\ $1.54$/$1.59$), Legal
Correctness ($0.55$), and Overall ($0.69$), reflecting how uniformly
Form documents land. Most strikingly, $31$ of $37$ Form documents
($83.8\,\%$) score Completeness $= 5$ together with Hallucination
(inverse) $\leq 2$, and $17$ Form documents combine the maximum
Completeness score of $5$ with the minimum Hallucination (inverse)
score of $1$ — Form documents are simultaneously the most
checklist-complete and the most fabricated.

The interpretation of these patterns against the research questions
is taken up in Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}.
